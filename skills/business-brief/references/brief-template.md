# business-brief.md template

Write the file in this order, with these headings. Other skills look for these headings by
name, so keep them exactly as written even when a section is thin.

Omit nothing. A section with no answer still gets a marker rather than being deleted —
a missing heading reads as "this business has no prices", which is different from "we did
not get to it".

## The three markers

Every unanswered field gets one of these, in square brackets. They look similar and mean
different things, and other skills act on the difference:

| Marker | Means | What another skill should do |
|---|---|---|
| `[withheld]` | They were asked and chose not to say | Work around it. Do not ask again |
| `[not asked]` | Nobody has asked yet | Ask, when it matters to the job at hand |
| `[gap]` | Asked, and there is genuinely no answer — or the sources disagree | Treat as unknown and say so in the output |

Never substitute one for another to make a brief look more finished.

---

```markdown
# <Business name>

<One sentence: what it does, for whom, where.>

Last updated: <date>

## What we sell

<One block per offer.>

### <Offer name>
- **Price:** <the real number, or one of the three markers>
- **What it is:** <a sentence or two>
- **Included:** <the specifics>
- **Not included:** <the specifics — this is the section proposals fail on>

<Repeat per offer. Mark the one they most want to sell with **(priority offer)**.>

## Who it is for

**The customer:** <in their words where possible>

**The problem before they call:** <what is actually going wrong for that person>

**What triggers the call:** <the event that makes them pick up the phone>

**A bad fit looks like:** <who to turn away, and why>

## Voice

**How we sound:** <two or three concrete characteristics, not adjectives>

**Never use these words:** <the list, verbatim>

**Formality:** <where they sit, in one line>

**Writing we were happy with:**

> <A real excerpt they supplied. Two or three of these are worth more than any
> description. Quote them exactly; do not tidy them up.>

## Proof

<Only what is true and usable. Real numbers, named clients cleared for reference,
results they can stand behind. If there is nothing solid, write `[none supplied]` —
that is a correct answer and stops other skills inventing statistics.>

## Constraints

**We do not:** <services they turn down, work outside their scope>

**Never claim:** <regulated or unsupportable claims>

**Capacity:** <what they can actually take on>

**Other:** <anything a document must never say>

## About the business

- **Operates in:** <geography>
- **Since:** <year>
- **People:** <number, and who does what if it matters>
- **Website:** <url>
```

## Notes for the skill, not for the file

- Keep the whole brief **under about 1,200 words**. It is loaded by every other skill;
  a long one costs the buyer money on every single call and makes the important parts
  harder to find.
- Prices and the never-use word list are the two highest-value sections. If the interview
  has to be cut short, get those.
- Excerpts under **Writing we were happy with** do more work than everything under
  **Voice** combined. Three real paragraphs beat a page of description.
- Mark every unanswered field with one of the three markers above, consistently, so the
  buyer can find them all with one search and other skills can detect them.
- Where a business runs several channels with different customers and pricing — a wholesale
  arm and a shop, say — record them as channels under **What we sell** and name the customer
  for each. Four channels at one address is not a four-item product range.
- Proof taken from the business's own website gets a year against it, or a note to verify.
