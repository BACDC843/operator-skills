# Worked example — a finished business-brief.md

This is what a good brief looks like when it is done. Show it to the user if they ask what
they are working towards, or if their own answers are coming back vague and an example
would move them along faster than another question.

The business is invented. Note three things about it, because they are the difference
between a brief that works and one that reads well and does nothing:

1. **The prices are real numbers.** Every skill that quotes, proposes or sells is worthless
   without them.
2. **The voice section is mostly quotation**, not description. Three sentences the owner
   actually wrote beat a page about their tone.
3. **There is a `[gap]` left in it on purpose.** A gap is a correct answer. Do not teach
   buyers to fill sections in with plausible-sounding invention.

---

```markdown
# Northgate Bookkeeping

Monthly bookkeeping for building trades — electricians, plumbers, roofers and small
builders — across Greater Manchester.

Last updated: 2026-08-16

## What we sell

### Monthly Books (priority offer)
- **Price:** £180/month for sole traders, £320/month up to 10 staff
- **What it is:** Every transaction reconciled monthly, VAT returns filed, a plain-English
  summary of where the money actually went
- **Included:** Bank reconciliation, VAT returns, supplier and customer ledgers, a
  fifteen-minute call each month, unlimited email questions
- **Not included:** Payroll, year-end accounts, anything to do with CIS deductions

### Payroll
- **Price:** £8 per payslip per month, £40/month minimum
- **What it is:** Payslips, RTI submissions, pension enrolment
- **Included:** Weekly or monthly runs, starters and leavers, P60s
- **Not included:** HR advice, contracts, disputes

### Catch-Up
- **Price:** £45/hour, quoted as a fixed price after a free look at the books
- **What it is:** For businesses that have not touched their books in months and have a
  deadline coming
- **Included:** Getting the records to a state where Monthly Books can start
- **Not included:** Negotiating with HMRC, penalty appeals

## Who it is for

**The customer:** Owner-operators in the building trades, one to ten staff. Usually the
person on the tools is also the person doing the invoicing at nine at night.

**The problem before they call:** The books are months behind, they do not know what they
have actually earned this year, and there is a deadline coming that they are quietly
worried about.

**What triggers the call:** A letter from HMRC, a VAT deadline two weeks out, or an
accountant asking for records they cannot produce.

**A bad fit looks like:** Anyone who needs audited accounts, anyone with more than about
fifteen staff, and anyone who wants the books "made to look a certain way". We have turned
down three of those and would again.

## Voice

**How we sound:** Direct and unbothered. We explain things once, in words a person on a
building site would use. We never make someone feel stupid for being behind — most of our
customers are behind, that is why they called.

**Never use these words:** solutions, leverage, seamless, robust, bespoke, journey,
partner (as a verb), holistic

**Formality:** The way you would talk to a good customer on the phone. First names,
contractions, full sentences.

**Writing we were happy with:**

> Your VAT return is filed. You owe £2,140 and it goes out on the 7th — I have put a
> reminder in for the 5th so it does not surprise you. Nothing else needs you this month.

> Being nine months behind is not a disaster and it is not unusual. It is about four hours
> of work and a fixed price. The only thing that makes it worse is leaving it another
> month, so let's get a date in.

> I would not bother with that. It would save you maybe £40 a year and cost you an
> afternoon of paperwork every quarter. Not worth your time.

## Proof

- 41 clients on Monthly Books, average tenure 3 years 2 months
- Zero late VAT filings in four years
- Named references cleared for use: R. Okonjo (Okonjo Electrical), S. Whitfield
  (Whitfield & Son Roofing)
- [gap] — we think average catch-up turnaround is about a fortnight but have never
  measured it, so do not claim a number

## Constraints

**We do not:** Give tax advice, prepare year-end accounts, handle CIS, or act as
registered agent for anything beyond VAT and PAYE.

**Never claim:** That we will reduce someone's tax bill. That we are accountants — we are
bookkeepers, and the distinction is regulated.

**Capacity:** Can take about four new Monthly Books clients a month before service slips.
Catch-Up work is limited to two at a time.

**Other:** Never name a client in marketing without written permission. Never mention a
client's figures, even anonymised — the trades in Manchester all know each other.

## About the business

- **Operates in:** Greater Manchester, plus remote clients anywhere in the UK
- **Since:** 2019
- **People:** 3 — Dana (owner, does the client-facing work), two part-time bookkeepers
- **Website:** northgatebookkeeping.co.uk
```

## Why this brief works

Point at these if the user asks what makes a good one:

- **A sales script written from it can quote £320/month** and say what is not included.
  That is the test. A brief that cannot survive it has not gathered enough.
- **"A bad fit looks like"** is sharper than the ideal-customer paragraph, and it took one
  question. Most owners have a much better answer for who to turn away.
- **The voice excerpts do the work.** "Direct and unbothered" is a description; *"Being
  nine months behind is not a disaster and it is not unusual"* is a demonstration, and a
  skill can match it.
- **The constraints section prevents specific, foreseeable harm** — a claim about reducing
  a tax bill, or calling a bookkeeper an accountant. Vague constraints prevent nothing.
- **The gap under Proof is the most important line in the document.** It is what stops
  every other skill inventing a turnaround time that sounds about right.
