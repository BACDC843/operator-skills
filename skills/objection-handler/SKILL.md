# Objection handler

The objections this offer actually attracts, and answers that are true.

An objection answered dishonestly closes one deal and loses the next three, because the
customer finds out and tells people. In a referral market that is the whole business.

## Read the brief

Look for `business-brief.md` in the working directory or the project's files.

- **Found:** read it. The price, the exclusions, the bad-fit answer and the constraints
  decide which objections are real and which answers are available.
- **Not found:** carry on. Ask for the offer, the price, and what people actually say.
  Mention `business-brief` once at the end.

## What it needs

- **The offer and its price.** Objections are priced. "Too expensive" at £180 a month and
  at £18,000 are different conversations.
- **What they actually hear**, if they know. Real objections beat predicted ones. Ask, and
  use theirs first.

If they have not noticed a pattern, derive the likely objections from the price point, the
exclusions and the bad-fit description — and say that is what you have done.

## Process

### 1. Separate the objection from the excuse

Most of what gets called an objection is a polite exit. "I need to think about it" is
usually "no" or "you have not told me something I needed". Say which is which, because they
need different responses and one of them needs none at all.

The honest answer to a polite exit is often to let them go.

### 2. Find the objection under the objection

"Too expensive" is almost never about the number. It is usually one of: they do not believe
it will work, they cannot see the cost of doing nothing, they have been burned before, or
it genuinely is too expensive for them and they are a bad fit.

Name which one each answer is aimed at. An answer aimed at the wrong underlying worry
sounds like pressure.

### 3. Write answers that are true

For each objection: what they say, what is usually underneath it, the answer in the
business's voice, and — where it applies — **when to agree with them instead**.

The last one is the part that makes this skill worth having. Some objections are correct.
If the brief says a bad fit looks like X and the person in front of them is X, the honest
answer is to say so and refer them elsewhere. Write those words.

### 4. Refuse the manipulative version

Do not write, and refuse if asked:

- False scarcity — deadlines, slot counts or price rises that are not real
- Manufactured urgency about consequences that will not happen
- Framing that makes the customer feel stupid for hesitating
- "What would it take to get you to yes today"
- Anything that survives only because the customer does not check

If the user asks for a harder line, say once why it costs more than it wins, and offer the
strongest honest version instead. Then write that.

### 5. Say what to fix upstream

Objections that keep coming up are usually a marketing problem, not a conversation problem.
If three of the objections would disappear with a clearer price page or an earlier
exclusion, say so in a line. That is worth more than the script.

## Output

Write to **`objections-<offer>.md`**.

```markdown
# Objections — <offer> at <price>

**Source:** <what the user reported hearing / derived from price and positioning>

## "<What they say>"

**Usually means:** <the worry underneath>

**Answer:**
> <the words, in their voice, short>

**Agree instead when:** <the case where this objection is correct, and what to say>

---

## Fix upstream
- <objection> would mostly disappear if <the change>

## Not written
- <anything refused, and why>
```

## Quality bar

**Good:** every answer is something the owner could still stand behind if the customer
repeated it back to them a year later.

**Failed:** answers that win the sentence and lose the trust. If an answer relies on the
customer not thinking about it for ten seconds, it is not an answer.

Never invent proof to break an objection — a statistic, a guarantee the business does not
offer, a client who did not exist. Never write a scarcity claim the user has not confirmed
is true. Close by saying what you could not do, and flag any objection you could not answer
honestly with the facts available.
