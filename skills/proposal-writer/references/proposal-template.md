# Proposal template

Use these headings in this order. Keep the whole thing to about two pages.

The order matters: their problem, then the work, then what is excluded, then the price.
Leading with the price makes it the only thing discussed; burying it looks evasive.

---

```markdown
# Proposal — <what the job is>

**For:** <client name>
**From:** <business name>
**Date:** <date>
**Valid until:** <date, only if the user confirmed a real shelf life — otherwise omit>

## What you asked for

<Their problem, in their words where you have them. Two short paragraphs at most. This
section exists to prove you listened, not to demonstrate expertise.>

## What we will do

### <Deliverable>
<One or two sentences. Something they could point at and say whether it arrived.>

**Timing:** <when, or [gap] if not given>

<Repeat per deliverable.>

## What is not included

<The section proposals fail on. Be specific and do not soften.>

- <Something discussed that is not in this price>
- <Something the client must supply, and by when>
- <Work the business does not do at all — from the brief's Constraints>

**If the scope changes:** <one plain sentence on what happens>

## Price

**<The number>**, <how it is structured — fixed, monthly, per unit>.

**Payable:** <when>

**This covers:** <one line>
**This does not cover:** <one line, pointing back up>

<If a range: what puts a job at each end. Never a decorative top figure.>

## Why us

<Two or three items from the brief's Proof section. Named clients only if cleared.
If Proof is [none supplied], delete this heading entirely rather than filling it.>

## Next steps

1. <Step, with a date>
2. <Step, with who does what>

<Contact details.>
```

---

## Notes for the skill, not for the document

- **Every `[gap]` must be listed back to the user** before they send. A proposal is the one
  document in this pack where an unfilled gap reaching a client costs real money.
- **Never write "anything not listed above" as an exclusion.** Name the two or three things
  that actually came up, or that this kind of job always attracts.
- **Delete headings that have nothing true to put under them.** An empty "Why us" is worse
  than none; a proposal that visibly reaches for credentials reads as a business without
  any.
- If the client asked for something the business does not do, say so in the proposal rather
  than quietly omitting it. Being told no in writing is better than discovering it later,
  and it is the cheapest trust the business will ever buy.
